# Market and competitors

Data: Apple's iTunes Search and Lookup API, 42 searches in 14 countries in each country's language, 2026-10-25.
"Ratings" means the number of ratings in that country's App Store.

## 1. The big picture
- **The need is real.** About half of people on long-term medicines don't take them as prescribed (WHO's 2003 adherence
  report; a review in Annals of Internal Medicine), and aging keeps growing the group on several medicines. Never
  turn these numbers into app claims (laws-and-safety.md §3).
- **The shelf is full.** Every country has the same global apps (Medisafe, MyTherapy), local pharmacy apps, and since
  2025 a wave of small AI-built apps translated into many languages: the same names (DoseHalo, Vitalyo, PillLabel,
  Dosivio, Mewdicate, PillsCheck, Recure, Hapche) appear in autocomplete in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian,
  Portuguese, Japanese and Korean.
- **Newcomers rarely get anywhere.** Medication apps in the search results (Medical or Health & Fitness, without period,
  contraception, symptom-checker and drug-price apps; pharmacy apps included):

confirm; says photos are discarded after reading), MedSnap (text reading plus AI for "every 8 hours"; iOS 18.6+),
MedLedger (AI prescription scanner; iOS 18.6+), DoseAlert (on-device text recognition; photos never stored or sent),

## 3. Apple Health is a competitor (free and built in)
- Medications in the Health app (iOS 16+): schedules (cycles and chosen days from iOS 18), dose reminders with a
  follow-up 30 minutes later, Taken / Skipped, Apple Watch logging, PDF export, sharing with family, a prompt to confirm
  the schedule after a time zone change. Press coverage reports critical alerts per medicine since iOS 17.
- **US only:** adding a medicine with the camera, and the interaction check.
- Other apps can **read** (not write) the medicines a person chooses to share (HealthKit Medications API, iOS 26).
- The redesigned Health app (Insights and Longevity tabs) is in the iOS 27.2 beta and ships "later this year"; no
  medication changes reported.
- **So:** in the US our scan competes with a free built-in feature; outside the US nothing built in scans.

## 4. What users complain about
From the store pages, Medisafe's own announcement and press coverage (first pass; README §4 has the full review task):
- **Basics behind a paywall:** "Now they want me to pay just to keep my reminders working" (a Medisafe user, quoted by
  Pillo, a competitor); free limits cut; $40 a year felt too much for what it does.
- **Too many taps to add a medicine,** confusing for people with "brain fog"; split tablets hard to enter.
- **Reminders that keep firing** after another family member logged the dose on their phone (sync bugs).
- **Notifications nobody asked for** (appointment prompts, add-ons) that can't be turned off.
- **Refill counts that ignore the schedule;** people want their own alert sounds.
- **Scans that can't be trusted** (Cadence review above).

What it means for the design: reminders free forever, adding a medicine in three taps, refill counts from the real
schedule, only the notifications the person set up (also guideline 4.5.4), and a check screen people trust.

## 5. Where we can be different (pick two or three and test them)
1. **The fastest way to add medicines outside the US:** photo, EU DataMatrix, German Medikationsplan, import from the
   Health app, in six languages, in the countries where Apple Health can't scan.
2. **Private by design:** no account, no ads, no analytics, works offline. The big apps can't copy this easily: their
   business runs on data and pharma partners.
3. **Warmth:** a character or a warm brand, like Pill Buddy and Max, and a kind tone for missed doses (no guilt).
   FaceRep's brand work (logo script, brand font, screenshot pipeline) carries over.
4. **Made for older eyes and their helpers:** very large text, a simple Today screen, family profiles on one phone; a
   remote caregiver link later.
5. **Honest pricing:** reminders free forever, a lifetime option.

**Niches seen in autocomplete** (each could become a focused version later): pets ("pet medication tracker"), ADHD,
HRT and menopause, the contraceptive pill, weekly GLP-1 injections, vitamins and supplements (big in Korea). Never put
drug brand names (Wegovy, Ozempic) or other apps' names in metadata (2.3.7).
